01Research question

What does BizMatch actually compete with?

BizMatch would compete first with trusted relationships and assembled workflows, not only with matching products.

The research tests the landscape and current behavior; it does not assume a unified platform is needed.

02Scope & methodology

36 companies. 22 substitute patterns. 34 evidence records. Four Jobs.

Company relationships, workflow substitutes, aggregate interviews, and active conclusions remain separate canonical layers.

Methodology and limitations
03Four Jobs-to-be-Done

Different actors, incentives, and trust requirements

01

Founder seeking a co-founder

Define the missing role; search prior relationships, professional profiles, communities and programs; use warm context to approach candidates; test compatibility through repeated interaction or shared work; verify references; then commit, hire instead, continue solo, defer or stop.

Detailed conclusion
02

Founder seeking an investor

Define the round and target criteria; assemble investors through databases, portfolio research, events, advisers and referrals; prioritize manually; request introductions or send targeted outreach; track replies; build credibility; disclose progressively; and continue into meetings, diligence and legal close.

Detailed conclusion
03

Investor sourcing startups or founders

Set a fund-specific mandate; source through professional networks, other investors, portfolio companies, proactive research, events, databases, scouts and inbound; triage in an internal funnel; meet and verify teams; then conduct fund-specific diligence and partner review.

Detailed conclusion
04

Founder and investor progressing from discovery to trusted interaction

Carry referral and conversation context into repeated contact; verify identity, claims and fit; build trust through references and behavior; stage disclosure from public context to controlled documents; use an NDA selectively; coordinate meetings; complete additional checks; and record a decision or next milestone.

Detailed conclusion

06Strongest substitutes

Behavior beats feature similarity

Prior relationships, warm introductions and shared work

They provide identity, reputation, reference context and observed behavior before a new platform has network density.

Conclusion confidence: Medium

Investor internal sourcing, selection and governance

The institutional funnel is mandate-specific and difficult for a generic self-service feed to replace.

Conclusion confidence: High

An assembled stack of familiar specialist tools

Users can retain control and avoid migration while replacing individual BizMatch capabilities.

Conclusion confidence: High

Human professionals and trusted intermediaries

Advisers, lawyers, accountants, mentors and existing investors combine context, accountability and judgment that software capability claims do not establish.

Conclusion confidence: Medium

Non-adoption and reversible fallback paths

A founder can hire, continue solo, bootstrap, defer, retain a manual process or stop. Evidence does not quantify how often or how satisfactory these choices are.

Conclusion confidence: Low
07Cross-market findings

What the evidence suggests

  1. Discovery is only one part of the work; the evidence does not establish that it is the primary failure.Selection, reference context, trust, verification, follow-through and disclosure occur after discovery and remain human- and context-intensive.Evidence
  2. Warm paths add context, but the evidence does not prove that they outperform algorithmic matching on partnership or investment outcomes.Introducers and prior relationships carry reputation and history; network exposure can also create bias and unequal access.Evidence
  3. Users can assemble a functional stack from specialized tools.Databases, email, spreadsheets, calendars, data rooms, e-signature and general AI cover bounded steps. Evidence of fragmentation exists, but evidence of dissatisfaction, switching intent and superior integrated outcomes is insufficient.Evidence
  4. Founder and investor Jobs are materially asymmetric.Founders seek access and response; investors manage scarce attention, mandate-specific selection and governance. A shared interface does not by itself create balanced incentives.Evidence
  5. Early NDA is context-dependent and may create friction in early VC interactions.Selective disclosure is a supported concern, but an NDA is not validated as the preferred universal trust mechanism.Evidence
08Assumptions strengthened

Trust, selective disclosure, and workflow continuity gained support

  • Trust and verification matter after initial discovery.That BizMatch can verify claims better than existing people and processes.
  • Selective disclosure is a real concern in some interactions.Severity, behavioral effect, preferred intervention and willingness to pay.
  • Workflow continuity can matter beyond a first match.That users want the continuity inside BizMatch rather than in their existing stack.
Counterevidence and trace
09Assumptions weakened

Discovery-first and one-marketplace assumptions weakened

  • Matching or discovery is the primary pain across the broad target market.Pain urgency within the narrower population of active co-founder seekers.
  • A mutual or AI match is more valuable than warm context and observed behavior.Incremental value of AI or mutual matching over curated introductions.
  • A pre-disclosure NDA is a broadly useful differentiator.Which user segments and disclosure moments benefit from an NDA gate.
  • One coherent marketplace can serve all four Jobs.Whether one narrowly defined entry Job can create value for both sides before expansion.
Supporting and opposing evidence
10What cannot yet be concluded

Current evidence does not justify a product or market verdict

  • BizMatch has product-market fit or validated demand.
  • A market gap, White Space or defensible beachhead has been established.
  • Users will switch from warm introductions or existing tool stacks.
  • Investors want another feed or will maintain another profile.
  • Either side will pay.
  • NDA, AI matching or swipe is a validated differentiator.
  • A broad MVP, final feature priority or Build/Buy decision is justified.
Full decision boundary
11Customer Discovery priorities

Test behavior, urgency, and switching—not feature preference

  1. P1Founder seeking a co-founder

    Among founders actively seeking a co-founder now, what triggered the search, what alternatives have they used, what happened, and what would make them change behavior?

  2. P1Investor sourcing startups or founders

    Which sourced opportunities receive attention, which are rejected, what trust signal changes the decision, and why would an investor add or ignore a new source?

  3. P1Founder and investor progressing to trusted interaction

    At which real interaction did trust, verification, disclosure or follow-through fail, and what action did each party take next?

  4. P2Founder seeking an investor

    During a recent raise, which tools and people were used from target selection through decision, where was context re-entered, and did that change an outcome?

  5. P2Founder and investor progressing to trusted interaction

    What information was withheld, from whom, until what event, and would an NDA have accelerated or delayed disclosure?
