Current research decision

Narrow the research

Exploratory research; not market validation

The existing material is useful exploratory research, but it does not constitute market validation for BizMatch.

  • Finding a co-founder was not the primary pain identified by the current interview sample.
  • The correct current recommendation is Narrow the research.
  • The research does not yet justify Proceed, Pivot, or Stop for the full venture.
Research scope

The current evidence primarily covers founder and co-founder discovery. Investor demand, project discovery, willingness to pay and the dynamics of a two-sided marketplace remain untested.

Secondary research assessment

What is useful

  • The research distinguishes between evidence, estimates, and information gaps.
  • It acknowledges missing Israeli data about active co-founder searches and willingness to pay.
  • It identifies liquidity, community-selection bias, and the difference between a match, a meeting, and a partnership.

What is unreliable or overstated

  • International platform activity does not validate urgent Israeli demand.
  • The TEA population is too broad to serve as BizMatch’s practical TAM.
  • The estimated SAM of 6,000–12,000 is based on weak proxy assumptions.
  • Employees are not a valid proxy for co-founder demand.
  • Business openings and business births are different measurements.
  • Conflicting GEM figures must not be used without identifying the correct source and year.
  • The previous 72/100 score creates false precision and must not be used as evidence supporting an MVP.

Scenario boundary: Any TAM, SAM, or SOM values in the existing material are scenarios, not validated forecasts.

Interpretation boundary

  • Two records were coded as actively looking for a partner; the question-level denominator is not documented.
  • Seven records were labelled Customers, Revenue, or Scale under the existing stage coding.
  • The sample did not match the intended Validation/MVP ICP.
  • Recruitment-source data was not consistently preserved.
  • Convenience-sample and personal-network bias are plausible.
  • The interviews cannot be treated as representative of Israeli founders.

Denominator boundary: These are record-level aggregate codes, not question-level prevalence estimates. Active-seeker eligibility, asked and answered counts were not preserved; venture stage and partner type require recoding. See active-seeker coding and stage coding in the evidence register.

Interview-methodology problems

  1. BizMatch was introduced before the participant’s problem was independently discovered.
  2. The interview primed participants to discuss partners, trust, and disclosure.
  3. Some raw questions directly introduced AI, swipe, NDA, and matching concepts.
  4. The KPI combined partner, investor, and trust problems even though they are different Jobs to Be Done.
  5. One record intentionally marked a participant as having a partner in order to continue through the interview flow.
  6. “Partner” was not consistently defined as co-founder, ownership partner, business partner, or collaborator.
  7. Personally identifying information was stored alongside research data without a documented anonymization and retention process.

Privacy boundary: This public report contains aggregate findings only. Participant names, contact details, recordings, transcripts, and other identifying information are intentionally excluded.

Evidence-use guidance

Keep as evidence

  • Raw recordings, JSON, and summaries in the private research repository.
  • Behaviour-based questions about previous cases, time, money, trust, and partner origin.
  • Negative findings whose question-level denominators have been audited.
  • Observations about reference checks, shared work, and selective disclosure.

Requires recoding

  • Co-founder versus other types of partners.
  • The intentionally incorrect interview record.
  • Venture stages.
  • Partner, investor, sales, and trust problems.
  • Recruitment source.
  • An anonymized analysis dataset.

Must not support decisions

  • The previous 72/100 score.
  • A combined “thesis fit” percentage.
  • Raw partner-ownership rates.
  • Urgency percentages that mix different problems.
  • Willingness-to-pay assumptions based on money spent solving other problems.
  • TAM/SAM/SOM as financial forecasts.
  • Treating the 15 interviews as representative.

Next Research Phase

“Narrow the research” means recoding the existing material first, then testing defined behavior with separate founder and investor protocols.

Phase A

Recode the 15 existing interviews

Tasks

  • Define a mutually exclusive partner taxonomy.
  • Correct the intentionally invalid partner-status record.
  • Audit every question denominator.
  • Separate co-founder, investor, sales, funding and trust problems.
  • Reclassify venture stage using a consistent rule.
  • Record recruitment source where known.
  • Mark unknown recruitment sources as unknown.
  • Produce an anonymized analysis dataset.
  • Preserve the raw records privately and never publish participant PII.

Deliverable: A recoded, anonymized dataset with a codebook, exclusion log and claim-level denominator audit.

Phase B

Focused founder discovery

Target participants

  • Validation/MVP founders.
  • Currently searching for a co-founder or having conducted a recent active search.
  • Recruited through more than the team’s immediate personal network.

Protocol requirements

  • Do not introduce BizMatch before independent problem discovery.
  • Ask about the participant’s most recent actual search.
  • Measure search duration, actions taken, alternatives used, outcomes and abandonment.
  • Distinguish warm introductions from platform-based discovery.
  • Record whether the participant is actively searching now.
  • Record recruitment source.
  • Avoid leading questions about swipe, AI, NDA or matching.
Phase C

Separate investor research

Protocol requirements

  • Use a separate research protocol for investors.
  • Do not combine co-founder discovery, investor deal flow, trust, fundraising, project discovery or willingness to pay into a single KPI.
  • Measure investor demand, sourcing workflow, current alternatives, screening effort, response quality and willingness to trial a new source independently.
Phase D

Behavioural concierge test

Test actual behaviour through curated manual introductions before building a broad MVP.

Measures

  • Profile completion.
  • Introduction acceptance.
  • Meeting scheduling.
  • Meeting completion.
  • Second meeting.
  • Trial-project or structured collaboration initiation.
  • Rejection reasons.
  • Disclosure-related delay.
  • Abandonment.
  • Time required to produce a viable introduction.

Measurement boundary: Profile views, stated interest and match acceptance are not equivalent to a successful partnership.

Phase E

Disclosure and NDA experiment

Comparison conditions

  • Staged disclosure without an NDA.
  • Optional NDA.
  • NDA required before detailed disclosure.

Measures

  • Actual continuation.
  • Delay.
  • Abandonment.

Measurement boundary: Do not ask only whether participants say they like NDAs.

Decision Gate Before Product Expansion

Decision rule: Numerical decision thresholds must be approved before recruitment begins. They must not be selected after seeing the results.

What remains unknown

Urgency among active co-founder seekers, dissatisfaction with alternatives, willingness to switch, NDA value and friction, investor demand, willingness to pay, and marketplace liquidity.

What could falsify the core hypothesis

A focused sample of active Validation/MVP founders may show that the problem is not urgent, warm introductions are sufficient, or users will not complete profiles and participate in curated introductions.

Current operating boundary

Allowed: Continue with narrower, hypothesis-driven research.

Not allowed: Claim that the need is validated, NDA is the differentiator, willingness to pay exists, the SAM is 6,000–12,000, or a broad MVP should be built.

Narrow the research

Metrics to collect

  • Question-level eligibility, asked, answered, missing, excluded and usable counts.
  • Recent search actions, duration, alternatives, outcomes and abandonment.
  • Profile completion, introduction acceptance, meetings, second meetings and structured collaboration.
  • Disclosure-related continuation, delay and abandonment.
  • Investor sourcing behavior, screening effort, response quality and willingness to trial a new source.
